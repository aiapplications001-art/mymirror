# Pre-Writing Strategy & Page Packet: Glycolic Acid for Acne Scars in India

## Target Persona
- **Demographics:** Indian men and women (18-35).
- **Pain Points:** Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH), atrophic scars, textured skin, recurrent acne.
- **Desired Outcomes:** Faded dark spots, smoother skin texture, safe exfoliation without aggravating dark skin.
- **Understanding Level:** Intermediate to advanced skincare knowledge. Knows about AHAs but seeks specific protocols for Indian skin types (Fitzpatrick IV-V) and products like Glyco 6, Glyco 12, Minimalist AHA 25%.

## Search Intent Gaps
- Competitors focus broadly on AHAs. Missing deep clinical context on molecular weight of glycolic acid and its rapid penetration which can cause PIH in Indian skin if not managed.
- Missing specific usage protocols for pharmacy products like Glyco 6% and Glyco 12% creams versus cosmetic toners.
- Need detailed breakdown of PIE (erythema) vs PIH (hyperpigmentation) and how glycolic acid impacts each differently.

## SERP Competitor Analysis
- **Healthline/WebMD:** Broad, generic. Lacks India-specific product recommendations.
- **Local Blogs (Nykaa, BeBeautiful):** Product-heavy, lacks clinical depth on chemical peels and molecular mechanisms.
- **Dermatologist sites:** Overly academic or just service pages.

## Clinical Section Blueprint
1. The Molecular Science of Glycolic Acid: Penetration Dynamics
2. Efficacy on Textured Scars vs Macular Discoloration
3. Glycolic Acid Toners vs. Pharmacy Creams (Glyco 6, Glyco 12) vs. Chemical Peels
4. The Risk of PIH in Indian Skin Types (Fitzpatrick IV-V)
5. Protocols for Acne Scars: Step-by-Step Guide
6. Deep Dive: Minimalist AHA 25% Peel vs In-Clinic Peels
7. FAQ Section
