Clinical Differential

The 3 Types of Small Bumps on Face: Comparison

Different bumps look similar under bathroom lighting but have completely different root causes and treatments.

Keratin & Sebum Plug

Closed Comedones (Whiteheads)

Core Cause: Sebum and dead skin cells plug the hair follicle entrance under intact skin.
Sensation: Non-itchy, rough sandpaper texture when running hands over face.
Typical Location: Cheeks, forehead, chin, and around the nose.
Inside the Bump: A firm, waxy plug of oxidized sebum and cellular keratin.
First-Line Treatment: 2% Salicylic Acid (Sebogel) + Adapalene
Yeast Overgrowth

Fungal Acne (Folliculitis)

Core Cause: Malassezia yeast overgrowth in follicles fed by sweat and lipids.
Sensation: Intense prickly itch that flares up after workouts or during monsoon humidity.
Typical Location: Hairline, upper forehead, temples, and upper chest.
Inside the Bump: Clear fluid or a sterile inflammatory papule (no waxy plug).
First-Line Treatment: Ketoconazole 2% Wash (5-min lather)
Keratin Pearl Seed

Milia Cysts

Core Cause: Trapped keratin flakes form tiny epidermal cysts under thin skin.
Sensation: Zero pain, zero itching; strictly a cosmetic texture concern.
Typical Location: Directly under the lower eyelids, temples, and upper cheeks.
Inside the Bump: A rock-hard miniature ball of keratin; impossible to squeeze.
First-Line Treatment: Gentle Retinoid or In-Clinic Unroofing

Root Causes

Why Indian Skin Develops Sub-Surface Facial Bumps

Environmental humidity, traditional hair care practices, and hard water residue combine to trap sebum beneath an intact stratum corneum.

1. Retention Hyperkeratosis & Closed Pore Entrances

Unlike open blackheads where the follicle opening dilates and oxidizes in air, closed comedones form when dead epidermal cells (corneocytes) fail to detach. They mix with thick squalene sebum, doming the overlying stratum corneum into a microscopic closed vault.

2. The Champi & Coconut Oil Yeast Trigger

Traditional overnight hair oiling (champi) with pure coconut, almond, or mustard oil transfers heavy fatty acids onto the forehead while sleeping. Coconut oil contains high levels of lauric and myristic acids, which Malassezia yeast feed upon voraciously, sparking widespread fungal folliculitis.

3. Trapped Keratin Seeds: How Milia Form

Milia are not acne lesions and contain zero sebum. They are tiny epidermal inclusion cysts formed when dead skin flakes become entrapped beneath fragile, thin skin—most commonly around the orbital eye area from rich, heavy eye creams or after sun damage.

4. Why Walnut Face Scrubs Multiply Bumps

Physical scrubs with jagged crushed walnut shells or apricot kernels lacerate the delicate epithelial domes covering closed comedones. This introduces surface bacteria into the trapped sebum plug, transforming painless microscopic bumps into painful inflammatory pustules.

Under The Skin

How Small Bumps Form Under Your Skin (And Why They Won't Pop)

See what happens inside a healthy pore versus a clogged closed comedone, and why squeezing only causes red, angry pimples.

Smooth Skin

Normal Open Pore (Clear Flow)

  • Natural Oil Exit: Sebum flows smoothly out of the pore to keep your face moisturized and protect your natural moisture barrier.
  • Effortless Shedding: Dead surface cells loosen and wash away naturally during cleansing, preventing build-up inside the pore canal.
  • Silky Smooth Feel: Because the follicle opening is clear and unclogged, your skin feels completely smooth, flat, and soft under your fingers.
Textured Bumps

Closed Comedone (Sealed Under Skin)

  • Sealed Roof (No Air): Unlike blackheads (which are open to air and oxidize black), closed comedones are trapped under an intact layer of skin. They stay flesh-colored or pale.
  • The Waxy Clog: Dead skin flakes fail to shed and mix with thick sebum, forming a firm waxy plug that gets trapped inside the follicle neck.
  • Why Squeezing Fails: Because there is no opening on top, pressing with your fingers ruptures the pore wall underneath, pushing bacteria into surrounding skin and creating an angry red pimple that leaves a dark scar.
Step 1

Dead Skin Flakes Stick

Dead surface cells fail to shed naturally. Instead of washing away, they clump together around the narrow pore neck.

Step 2

The Waxy Seed Forms

Fresh oil from sebaceous glands gets trapped behind the stuck skin flakes, hardening into an invisible microscopic plug.

Step 3

Trapped Under Intact Skin

The plug grows, but the pore mouth remains covered by an intact layer of skin. Without air exposure, it stays pale rather than turning black.

Step 4

The Sandpaper Bump

The trapped pocket pushes upward, creating a 1–3mm flesh-colored bump that feels like sandpaper under direct light.

4-Week Recovery Roadmap

The 4-Week Texture Clearing Roadmap

Step-by-step dermatological guidelines to transition from rough texture to smooth glass skin.

Phase 01 • Week 1

Intra-Follicular Decongestion

Introduce Sebogel (2% Salicylic Acid) 3 nights a week. Salicylic acid is oil-soluble, penetrating deep into the sebum-filled pore to break desmosomal bonds holding dead keratin scales together.

Phase 02 • Week 2

Yeast Suppression & Champi Halt

For forehead bumps, lather with Nizral 2% shampoo twice a week, allowing the ketoconazole foam to sit for 5 minutes before rinsing. Stop all coconut and almond hair oils immediately.

Phase 03 • Week 3

Cellular Turnover Normalization

Begin applying a pea-sized dot of Adapalene 0.1% on alternate nights over moisturizer. Adapalene binds to nuclear retinoic acid receptors, normalizing epithelial differentiation to prevent new microcomedones.

Phase 04 • Week 4+

Non-Comedogenic Hydration

Support stratum corneum hydration with oil-free gel creams containing niacinamide and hyaluronic acid. Protect healing skin with 100% mineral zinc oxide sunscreen to prevent post-acne marks.
