1. How Glycolic Acid Face Washes Exfoliate Surface Hyperpigmentation

Glycolic Acid, derived from sugar cane, is the smallest molecule in the alpha-hydroxy acid (AHA) family. This tiny molecular size allows it to penetrate the epidermis efficiently, cleaving the ionic bonds between pigmented keratinocytes (dead skin cells holding excess melanin).

By loosening these bonds, it promotes the shedding of superficial dark spots and uneven texture without causing deep, systemic irritation often associated with mechanical scrubs. This process of chemical desquamation accelerates cell turnover, revealing brighter, more even-toned skin underneath.

2. Glycolic Acid (AHA) Wash vs Salicylic Acid (BHA) Wash: Which Do You Need?

Understanding the difference between AHA and BHA is crucial for targeting specific skin concerns in Indian skin types.

AHA (Glycolic Acid)

Water-soluble. Works on the skin's surface. Best for fading surface dark spots, improving rough texture, and treating dry or dull skin. It cannot penetrate through oil.

BHA (Salicylic Acid)

Oil-soluble. Can bypass sebum to penetrate deep inside the pore lining. Best for clearing deep pore oil, dissolving blackheads, and treating active, inflammatory acne.

4. The 60-Second Contact Rule for AHA Cleansers

Unlike leave-on serums, face washes are quickly rinsed off. To maximize desquamation (the shedding of dead skin cells), you must follow the 60-Second Contact Rule.

Massage the cleanser gently onto damp skin for a full 60 seconds. This gives the Glycolic Acid molecules enough time to penetrate the upper layers of the stratum corneum and loosen the bonds of pigmented cells before being washed down the drain. Do not scrub harshly!

5. ⚠️ Rebound PIH Warning: Over-Exfoliation Signs on Indian Skin

Indian skin (Fitzpatrick III-V) is prone to Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH). While Glycolic Acid is intended to fade dark spots, overusing it can trigger inflammation that actually causes more pigmentation.

🚨 Signs of Over-Exfoliation

  • The "Glassy" Look: Skin looks overly tight, shiny, or stretched.
  • Stinging on Application: Your regular moisturizer suddenly burns or stings.
  • Increased Redness: Persistent erythema that wasn't there before.
  • Rebound Darkening: Existing dark spots appear darker due to inflammation.

6. How to Layer Glycolic Face Wash with Niacinamide & Sunscreen

A well-structured routine is the key to fading dark spots safely without compromising your skin barrier.

Step 1: Cleanse

Use your Glycolic Acid face wash for 60 seconds (PM routine preferred). Rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water.

Step 2: Niacinamide

Apply a soothing 5% Niacinamide serum on damp skin. Niacinamide repairs the barrier, reduces redness, and blocks melanin transfer, perfectly complementing the AHA wash.

Step 3: Moisturize

Lock in hydration with a ceramide-rich moisturizer to prevent trans-epidermal water loss (TEWL).

Step 4: Sunscreen (AM)

In the morning, apply a broad-spectrum SPF 50 PA++++ sunscreen generously. AHA makes skin photosensitive!
