1 The Pharmacology: RAR Receptor Selectivity & Comedolytic Action

Outpatient clinical protocols across Indian tertiary dermatology centers require careful retinization scheduling when initiating topical adapalene for acne vulgaris. Adapalene is a third-generation synthetic naphthoic acid derivative that selectively binds to retinoic acid receptors RAR-beta and RAR-gamma within follicular keratinocytes. Unlike first-generation all-trans retinoic acid, adapalene exhibits minimal affinity for cytosolic retinoic acid-binding proteins or RAR-alpha, significantly diminishing cutaneous peeling and burning while preserving high comedolytic potency. In cutaneous pharmacology, adapalene normalizes follicular infundibular desquamation, clearing subclinical microcomedones before inflammatory rupture occurs. Furthermore, adapalene inhibits arachidonic acid lipoxygenase metabolism and down-regulates Toll-like receptor 2 expression on monocytes, delivering rapid anti-inflammatory cytokine suppression. For acute comedonal and papulopustular flares, dermatologists prescribe pure adapalene 0.1% formulations (Galderma Differin 0.1% gel, INR 380 to 450 per 15g tube; Glenmark Adaferin 0.1% gel, INR 320 to 395 per 15g tube) or fixed-dose combinations with clindamycin (Glenmark Deriva-CMS, INR 320 to 395 per 15g tube) utilized on alternate evenings. To buffer stratum corneum lipid bilayers, clinicians mandate pre-treatment barrier priming with physiological ceramide emulsions (Cipla Excela Max, INR 480 to 540 per 50g) maintaining transepidermal water loss below 12 g/m²/h across Fitzpatrick phototypes IV through VI. Compare the clinical diagnostic performance of topical acne retinoids across sensitive South Asian phototypes: microcomedone clearance rate: superior follicular desquamation vs slower chemical peeling. Cutaneous tolerance profile: minimal facial erythema and flaking compared to tretinoin 0.025% topical cream formulations. Chemical photostability: completely stable under fluorescent illumination and compatible with concomitant benzoyl peroxide application. Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation risk: low incidence of secondary epidermal pigment darkening when layered over physiological lipid barrier moisturizers. 2 Clinical Retinization Protocols & The Sandwich Method

Outpatient dermatologists enforce a structured retinization protocol across Indian retail pharmacies and clinical dispensaries to prevent retinoid dermatitis on melanin-rich skin. During weeks one through four, patients apply a pea-sized quantity of adapalene 0.1% two evenings per week using the sandwich method: applying ceramide lotion first, allowing twenty minutes latency, applying adapalene, and sealing with barrier cream. Outpatient dermatology consensus guidelines mandate combining topical retinoids with fixed-dose benzoyl peroxide (Galderma Epiduo gel, adapalene 0.1% + BPO 2.5%, INR 750 to 920 per 15g tube) for recalcitrant pustular lesions, or pairing with short-contact 2.5% benzoyl peroxide bars (Perobar 2.5% bar, INR 140 to 195 per 75g bar). For post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation management, clinicians introduce azelaic acid (Micro Labs Aziderm 10% to 15% gel, INR 280 to 360 per 15g tube) on alternate mornings alongside broad-spectrum matte sunscreen SPF 50+ to inhibit melanocyte stimulation across Fitzpatrick phototypes IV through VI.
