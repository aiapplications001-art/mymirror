4. The Right Concentration: 10% vs 15% vs 20% Compared

Walking into a pharmacy or shopping online, you will see bottles labeled 10%, 15%, and 20% Vitamin C. It is easy to assume higher numbers fade marks faster. On Indian skin, that assumption often backfires into stinging, redness, and fresh breakouts.

Indian skin has reactive pigment cells (melanocytes). When a serum is overly acidic or too concentrated, it triggers micro-inflammation. Your skin responds to that irritation by producing extra dark pigment (rebound PIH). Here is how dermatologists break down the percentages:

5. The 3 Active Molecules: Popular Examples, When to Apply & How to Use Each

The percentage on the bottle is only half the story. The chemical form of Vitamin C dictates how stable it is, whether it turns orange on your vanity, and how deeply it penetrates without stinging:

1. Pure L-Ascorbic Acid (LAA)

The Direct Biological Active

The purest and most biologically potent form. It does not require cellular conversion; your skin uses it immediately. However, it requires a very low acidic pH (3.0–3.5) and oxidizes rapidly in Indian heat and humidity.

Product Example: Foxtale 15% L-Ascorbic Acid Serum (uses an airless pump).

When to Apply: Morning under sunscreen, or night on completely dry skin.

How to Store: Keep in a cool, dark cabinet or refrigerator to prevent turning brown.

2. 3-O-Ethyl Ascorbic Acid (EAA) ⭐

The Modern Heat-Stable Favorite

The gold-standard derivative favored by Indian dermatologists. It has an ethyl group shielding the molecule from heat up to 40°C. It penetrates easily at a skin-friendly pH (4.5–5.5) and converts into active Vitamin C in the skin with virtually zero stinging.

Product Examples: Minimalist 10%, Dr. Sheth’s 10%, Plum 15%, Deconstruct 10%.

When to Apply: Every morning on clean, dry face before moisturizer and SPF 50.

How to Store: Stable at room temperature in dark amber glass bottles.

3. Sodium Ascorbyl Phosphate (SAP)

The Gentle Anti-Acne Derivative

A stable, water-soluble salt derivative. It operates at neutral pH (around 6.0 to 7.0) and offers natural antimicrobial benefits against acne-causing bacteria (*Cutibacterium acnes*), making it ideal if you get frequent breakouts.

Product Examples: Acne-friendly beginner formulations and calming spot treatments.

When to Apply: Morning or evening; excellent when actively dealing with small pustules.

How to Store: Highly photostable; store at ordinary room temperature.

Why Derivatives Win in India

The Heat & Melanin Equation

In European climates, refrigerated LAA works well. But in Indian summers, sweat and acidity cause low-pH formulas to burn. Derivatives like EAA release Vitamin C gradually right next to melanocytes, clearing marks without triggering rebound dark pigment.
