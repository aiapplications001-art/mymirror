The Biochemical Myth of "Niacinamide Purging"

A common misconception among skincare consumers in India is that breakout bumps occurring after starting Niacinamide are a sign of "skin purging". In dermatology, this is biochemically impossible.

SYSTEM CLINICAL LOG: CELLULAR TURNOVER AUDIT
[PURGE TRIGGER]: Actives that increase cell turnover (Retinoids, Glycolic, Salicylic).
[NIACINAMIDE MOLECULE]: Vitamin B3 amide (Nicotinamide). Zero desmosomal dissolution properties.
[DIAGNOSIS]: Niacinamide DOES NOT exfoliate skin or surface micro-comedones.
[CONCLUSION]: Any bump from Niacinamide is Micro-Irritation or Nicotinic Acid Flushing—NOT Purging.

10% Osmotic Stress vs. 5% Ceramide Synthesis

Compare the physiological impact of 10% high-dose serums against the dermatologically proven 5% concentration:

10% Concentration (High Risk)

Osmotic Disruption: Concentrated 10% solutions draw water out of epidermal keratinocytes, creating cell tightness and micro-tears.

Nicotinic Acid Flush: Residual unconverted nicotinic acid triggers cutaneous vasodilation, itching, and red inflammatory hives on Indian skin.

2% – 5% Concentration (Sweet Spot)

Ceramide Synthesis: Stimulates endogenous synthesis of Ceramides 1, 3, and 6-II in the stratum corneum, locking in moisture.

Melanosome Inhibition: Blocks pigment transfer from melanocytes to surface cells, fading stubborn Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH).
