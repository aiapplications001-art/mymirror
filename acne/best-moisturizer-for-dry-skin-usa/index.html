1. The Stratum Corneum Barrier: Why Dry Skin Keeps Getting Drier

To permanently eliminate dry skin, you must first understand the structural architecture of the outer skin layer—the stratum corneum. In dermatological science, the stratum corneum is modeled as a brick-and-mortar system. The "bricks" are dead, keratinized skin cells called corneocytes, while the "mortar" is a complex intercellular lipid matrix composed of three essential lipids: ceramides, cholesterol, and free fatty acids in an exact 3:1:1 molar ratio.

When this 3:1:1 lipid balance is compromised—whether by harsh surfactant face washes, natural aging, genetic eczema predispositions, or environmental stressors—the structural mortar crumbles. Microscopic cracks open across the epidermis, causing Transepidermal Water Loss (TEWL) to skyrocket. Hydration rapidly evaporates from the deeper dermal layers into the air, resulting in chronic tightness, flaking, stinging, and redness.

❄️ Critical US Climate Context: Winter Heating & TEWL Spikes

In North America, central forced-air heating during winter months drops indoor relative humidity to an extreme 15%–20% (far below the dermatologically optimal range of 40%–60%). This bone-dry indoor air acts like a sponge, pulling water directly out of compromised stratum corneum lipids. As a result, dry skin is a near-universal winter crisis across North American climate zones (Northeast, Midwest, and Mountain West).

Simply splashing water onto dry skin or applying light gel lotions only provides temporary relief because without occlusive lipids, that moisture evaporates instantly—taking additional intrinsic water with it and leaving the skin drier than before. True barrier restoration requires lipid-replenishing creams rich in physiological ceramides and high-potency occlusives.

4. Slugging: The Petrolatum Trend for Dry US Skin

"Slugging" has evolved from a viral skincare trend into a dermatologically validated evening protocol for severely dry, dehydrated, or barrier-compromised skin. The process involves applying a paper-thin layer of 100% white petrolatum ointment (such as Vaseline Healing Jelly, CeraVe Healing Ointment, or Aquaphor) as the absolute final step in your nighttime routine.

Clinical Efficacy: Pharmacological studies confirm that white petrolatum is the most powerful occlusive agent available over-the-counter. It creates a temporary hydrophobic membrane over the stratum corneum that reduces Transepidermal Water Loss (TEWL) by up to 98%, giving the underlying skin lipids an optimal moisture-rich environment to heal overnight.

⚠️ Important Dermatological Warnings on Slugging

Do NOT slug if you have active acne or comedone-prone skin. While pure petrolatum is non-comedogenic (molecules are too large to enter pores), its occlusive seal can trap sebum, dead skin cells, and Cutibacterium acnes bacteria. Furthermore, never slug over strong active treatments like prescription retinoids (Tretinoin, Adapalene) or AHAs/BHAs, as occlusion dramatically enhances active penetration and can lead to severe chemical burns or contact dermatitis.

The Step-by-Step PM Slugging Protocol for Dry Skin

Step 1

Gentle Hydrating Cleanse

Wash face with a non-foaming, lotion-based cleanser (e.g. CeraVe Hydrating Cleanser or Vanicream Gentle Facial Cleanser) using lukewarm water. Do not scrub or strip skin barrier lipids.

Step 2

Hydrating Serum (Damp Skin)

Apply 2–3 drops of a pure Hyaluronic Acid serum or hydrating essence onto slightly damp skin to maximize water attraction into upper epidermal layers.

Step 3

Ceramide Barrier Cream

Smooth a generous layer of CeraVe Moisturizing Cream over face and neck to supply physiological ceramides, cholesterol, and fatty acids to the stratum corneum.

Step 4

Thin Petrolatum Film (Slug)

Warm a pea-sized dot of 100% white petrolatum ointment between your fingertips and gently press a paper-thin film over dry zones to seal in all underlying hydration overnight.

6. Frequently Asked Questions About Dry Skin Moisturizers in the US

Dermatologists recommend CeraVe because of its patented MVE slow-release delivery system and its inclusion of 3 essential ceramides (1, 3, 6-II) in physiological ratios. These ingredients repair the natural skin barrier rather than merely greasing the surface, and the products carry the NEA Seal while remaining affordable and HSA/FSA eligible across US retailers.

While CeraVe provides superior barrier-repairing ceramides, Vanicream offers unmatched purity for skin that reacts to almost everything else.

You should do it if you have severely dry, flaky skin or a compromised barrier. However, skip slugging if you have active acne or oily, comedone-prone skin.

For dry skin, HA serums must always be followed by an occlusive moisturizer.
