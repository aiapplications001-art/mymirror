1. Clinical Overview & Background

Alpha Hydroxy Acids (AHAs) are chemical exfoliants that loosen desmosome bonds between dead skin cells. However, Indian skin (Fitzpatrick IV-VI) has higher epidermal melanin density, making active selection critical to prevent post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH).

2. Quick Verdict: Which Active Should You Pick?

Clinical Active Recommendation

Choose Glycolic Acid 5%-10% if your skin barrier is resilient and your main concern is deep sun spots, stubborn post-acne marks, or rough skin texture. Choose Lactic Acid 5%-10% if you have sensitive, dry, or melanin-dense skin, as it provides gentle exfoliation with inherent NMF humectant hydration.

3. Dermatological Action Mechanisms & Science

Glycolic Acid has a small molecular weight (76.05 g/mol), allowing rapid penetration through outer epidermal layers. Lactic Acid has a larger molecular size (90.08 g/mol) that works on the surface stratum corneum, significantly reducing the risk of stinging or chemical burns.

Glycolic Acid Deep Penetration

Smallest AHA molecule. Dissolves intercellular cement binding dead keratinocytes, accelerating cell renewal.

Lactic Acid Humectant AHA

Larger AHA molecule derived from fermented milk sugars. Exfoliates gently while binding water to skin cells.

4. Decision Criteria for Indian Skin Types

Dermatologists recommend starting AHA exfoliants 1 to 2 nights per week. Always pair acid usage with a broad-spectrum SPF 50 sunscreen every morning to prevent UV rays from darkening newly exposed skin cells.
