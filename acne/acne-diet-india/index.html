Pathophysiology of Dietary Acne: Endocrine Drivers and Pilosebaceous Distribution

In outpatient dermatological practice across India, dietary acne in adult females manifests primarily across the mandibular jawline and chin zones. Pilosebaceous units on the lower face demonstrate high densities of 5-alpha reductase type I enzymes, which convert circulating free testosterone and dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate (DHEA-S) into active dihydrotestosterone (DHT). Elevated intracellular DHT binds nuclear androgen receptors within basal sebocytes, triggering sebum hypersecretion and follicular hyperkeratosis. In adult women experiencing polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) or luteal phase progesterone surges, elevated sebum production combines with abnormal infundibular corneocyte desquamation, generating dense microcomedones that rapidly transition into inflammatory papulopustules. Outpatient clinical protocols combine topical 2% salicylic acid body washes (Saslic DS wash, INR 290 to 440 per bottle) with targeted 2.5% benzoyl peroxide bars (Perobar 2.5%, INR 140 to 195) to suppress bacterial proliferation and resolve microcomedones.

Friction from compressive sports bras and synthetic polyester athletic garments induces acute acne mechanica across the thoracic spine. When friction coincides with ambient monsoon humidity and unevaporated eccrine sweat, stratum corneum corneocytes swell, obstructing follicular ostia within 45 minutes of sustained athletic exertion. Trapped perspiration solutes and surface lipids create an anaerobic microenvironment favoring rapid Cutibacterium acnes proliferation and secondary inflammatory follicular rupture across delicate sensitive facial skin planes.

Differential Clinical Diagnosis: Dietary Acne Vulgaris vs Malassezia Folliculitis

Outpatient clinical dermatologists differentiate inflammatory acne vulgaris from fungal Malassezia folliculitis (Pityrosporum folliculitis) using polarized contact dermoscopy and Wood's lamp examination. Acne vulgaris presents with polymorphic lesions including open blackheads, closed microcomedones, and tender inflammatory nodules distributed irregularly across the mandibular jawline. Conversely, Malassezia folliculitis manifests as uniform monomorphic erythematous 1 to 2 mm follicular papulopustules accompanied by intense pruritus, typically triggered by tropical heat and antibiotic-induced microbial dysbiosis.

Outpatient Clinical Therapeutics and Low-Glycaemic Nutritional Protocols

In outpatient dermatological practice across India, board-certified dermatologists prescribe short-contact bactericidal washes to eliminate deep follicular Cutibacterium acnes colonization while avoiding textile bleaching. Applying a 2.5% to 5% benzoyl peroxide wash (Perobar 2.5% bar, INR 140 to 195) with a three-minute active contact latency induces rapid lipoperoxidation of bacterial cell membranes without selecting for resistant strains. For comedonal and hyperkeratotic truncal lesions, patients incorporate a 2% salicylic acid wash (Saslic DS wash, INR 290 to 440 per bottle) to dissolve oxidized squalene and desquamate cohesive corneocyte plugs. Nighttime topical retinoid therapy utilizes adapalene 0.1% microsphere gel (Deriva-MS, INR 280 to 380 per 15g tube), which selectively binds nuclear retinoic acid receptors (RAR-beta and RAR-gamma) within follicular infundibula, downregulating toll-like receptor 2 (TLR-2) and suppressing post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. Following therapeutic cleansing, applying a physiological 3:1:1 multi-lamellar ceramide emulsion (Cipla Excela Max, INR 480 to 540 per 50g) reinforces intercellular lipid bilayers, maintaining transepidermal water loss below 12 g/m²/h across the posterior thoracic skin plane. Clinical protocols demonstrate high compliance when incorporating ceramide moisturizers alongside active washes.
