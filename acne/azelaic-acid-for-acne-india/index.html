If you have tried salicylic acid washes or harsh scrubs for acne only to end up with dry, flaky skin and dark spots that refuse to fade, you are not alone. Many acne treatments dry out your skin barrier while doing little for the red or brown marks left behind.

Azelaic acid works differently. It is a gentle yet active compound that tackles three major skin issues at once: it calms swelling, reduces acne-causing bacteria, and prevents extra melanin production. This makes it particularly effective for Indian skin, which often reacts to breakouts by developing stubborn brown pigmentation.

Whether you are considering a 10% over-the-counter serum or a 15% pharmacy gel like Aziderm, here is everything you need to know to use it safely and see clear results.

1. How Azelaic Acid Heals Acne and Dark Spots

Azelaic acid is a naturally occurring compound that delivers triple-action benefits for acne-prone skin:

  • Slows Down Excess Melanin: When a pimple heals on Indian skin, pigment cells often dump too much melanin, leaving a flat dark mark known as post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH). Azelaic acid targets only the overactive pigment cells, helping marks fade without lightening the surrounding skin.
  • Calms Redness and Swelling: It has soothing properties that cool down hot, inflamed red bumps and reduce facial flushing in rosacea-prone skin.
  • Clears Trapped Pores: It gently encourages dead skin cells to shed naturally while reducing the bacteria that cause breakouts, preventing new pimples from forming.
Pregnancy and Nursing Friendly: Most acne medications, including tretinoin and oral pills, must be avoided during pregnancy. Azelaic acid holds an FDA Category B rating, making it one of the rare active ingredients dermatologists recommend as safe for expectant and nursing mothers.

2. 10% OTC vs. 15% Pharmacy Gel: Which One Do You Need?

In Indian pharmacies and beauty stores, azelaic acid comes in two main categories: 10% cosmetic products and 15% pharmacy-grade gels.

10% Cosmetic Formulations Beginners

Products like Minimalist 10% or Chemist at Play are gentle starting points. They help brighten mild dullness and even out tone with low risk of stinging. Ideal for sensitive skin newcomers.

15% Pharmacy Gels Clinical Grade

Medicines like Aziderm 15% Gel or Picspot 15% provide the concentration tested in dermatology clinical trials for moderate acne, persistent redness, and deep brown marks.

Why Gel Formulation Matters on Indian Skin: Heavy creams and ointments often contain thick oils that can clog pores in humid Indian climates. A lightweight micro-gel vehicle absorbs cleanly into the skin without leaving a greasy film or triggering blackheads.

4. The Dermatologist Secret: Pairing Azelaic Acid with Retinoids

One of the most effective regimens for stubborn acne and marks is combining azelaic acid with a topical retinoid like adapalene or tretinoin. However, putting them on at the exact same moment can cause peeling and barrier damage.

The AM / PM Split Routine:
  • Morning: Cleanse with water or gentle wash → Apply a pea-sized amount of 15% Azelaic Acid Gel → Moisturizer → SPF 50 Sunscreen.
  • Night: Gentle cleanser → Light moisturizer → Apply a pea-sized amount of your Retinoid (Adapalene or Tretinoin) on dry skin.

This split routine allows azelaic acid to calm inflammation during the day, while your retinoid speeds up cellular renewal overnight. Always apply sunscreen daily, as both ingredients make skin more sun-sensitive.

5. Why Does It Tingle or Itch? (And How to Stop It)

When starting azelaic acid, almost everyone experiences a distinct prickling, tingling, or itching sensation for 10 to 15 minutes after application. This is completely expected.

Because azelaic acid requires an acidic environment to penetrate skin cells, it activates surface sensory nerves. This is not an allergic reaction, and the sensation typically disappears after 10 to 14 days of consistent use.

The Sandwich Buffer Technique

If the tingling feels uncomfortable, use the buffering method:

  1. Wash your face and pat it completely dry. (Applying acids to damp skin increases tingling).
  2. Apply a thin layer of a gentle barrier moisturizer and wait 5 minutes.
  3. Dot a pea-sized amount of azelaic acid over the moisturizer.
  4. Start by applying it every second night. Once your skin feels comfortable, you can move to nightly use.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Azelaic Acid safe during pregnancy and breastfeeding? +

Yes. Azelaic acid has an FDA Category B rating. It is one of the few acne and mark-fading treatments considered safe by doctors during pregnancy and nursing, making it an alternative when retinoids cannot be used.

Can you use Azelaic Acid and Vitamin C together? +

Yes, but using them at the same time can irritate sensitive skin. Many people apply Vitamin C in the morning for antioxidant defense and Azelaic Acid at night, or alternate them on different days.

Why does my skin tingle or itch when I start Azelaic Acid? +

Azelaic acid works best at a lower pH level (around 4.0), which can stimulate surface nerve endings and cause a prickly or itchy feeling for 10 to 15 minutes. This usually settles within two weeks as your skin adapts.

Should you choose 10% OTC or 15% pharmacy gel? +

Beginners and people with easily irritated skin often start with 10% over-the-counter serums. For persistent acne, redness, or dark marks, 15% pharmacy gels like Aziderm deliver stronger results and absorb cleanly on oily skin.

Do you apply Azelaic Acid before or after moisturizer? +

If you feel itching or stinging, put your moisturizer on first, wait 5 minutes, and then apply the azelaic acid on top. If your skin is comfortable and adjusted, applying it to clean, dry skin under your moisturizer gives the best results.

How long does it take to fade dark acne spots (PIH)? +

Azelaic acid slows down overactive pigment cells. Most people see noticeable fading of brown and dark acne marks after 8 to 12 weeks of daily application combined with sunscreen.
