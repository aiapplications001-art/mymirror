Pathophysiology of Post-Acne Erythema (PIE)

Post-Acne Erythema (PIE) consists of flat, pink-to-bright red spots left behind after inflammatory papules or cysts resolve. Unlike brown/black Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH), PIE is entirely vascular in origin.

During acne inflammation, surrounding micro-capillaries in the superficial papillary dermis dilate and undergo wall damage. Even after bacteria die, these capillaries remain locked in a chronically dilated state.

Capillary Damage Recovery Stages

Track micro-vascular healing across the 3 recovery phases:

Phase 1: Acute Vascular Leakage Days 1–14

Capillary permeability spikes, releasing plasma into papillary tissue under high local VEGF expression.

Phase 2: Chronic Vasodilation Weeks 2–8

Capillaries remain structurally enlarged. Erythema appears bright pink or purplish under surface epidermis.

Phase 3: Vessel Remodeling Weeks 8–16

Tranexamic acid constricts vessel lumen while Centella triterpenes stimulate Type III collagen around vessel walls.

Tranexamic 3% + Centella Layering Protocol

Combine VEGF vascular suppression with vessel wall structural repair for maximum red spot clearance:

1. Morning Application: Tranexamic Acid 3%

Apply 3 to 4 drops of 3% Tranexamic Acid serum on clean skin to inhibit plasminogen activator and constrict dilated micro-capillaries.

2. Evening Application: Centella Asiatica (Cica 5%)

Follow with a rich Cica gel moisturizer. Madecassoside stimulates Type III collagen synthesis around fragile dermal capillary walls.

Frequently Asked Questions

Post-Acne Erythema (PIE) is caused by damaged, permanently dilated micro-capillaries in the papillary dermis, not melanin pigment. Tyrosinase inhibitors like Vitamin C target melanocytes and PIH, but have zero effect on vascular capillary red spots.
Tranexamic Acid inhibits plasminogen activator, suppressing vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) synthesis and reducing micro-vascular permeability to constrict dilated capillaries.
