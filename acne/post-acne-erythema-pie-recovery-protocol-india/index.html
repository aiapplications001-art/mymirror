Pathophysiology of Post-Acne Erythema (PIE)

Post-Acne Erythema (PIE) consists of flat, pink-to-bright red spots left behind after inflammatory papules or cysts resolve. Unlike brown/black Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH), PIE is entirely vascular in origin.

1. Micro-Capillary Damage & Telangiectasia

During acute acne inflammation, surrounding micro-capillaries in the superficial papillary dermis dilate and undergo structural wall damage. Even after bacteria are destroyed, these capillaries remain locked in a chronically dilated state (erythema).

2. The Glass Slide (Blanching) Test

Press a clear glass slide firmly against the red mark. If the spot temporarily disappears (blanches) under pressure, it is PIE (vascular blood flow). If it remains dark, it is PIH (melanin pigment deposits).

Crucial Skincare Misconception: Applying Vitamin C, Glycolic Acid, or Hydroquinone to PIE red marks will NOT work because tyrosinase inhibitors target melanin cells, whereas PIE requires micro-vascular constriction and capillary wall repair.

Micro-Capillary Damage Recovery Phases

Understand the 3 physiological stages of capillary healing in dermal tissue:

Phase 1 (Days 1–14)
Acute Vascular Leakage

Capillary permeability spikes, releasing plasma and red blood cells into papillary tissue. High local VEGF expression.

Phase 2 (Weeks 2–8)
Chronic Vasodilation

Capillaries remain structurally enlarged. Erythema appears bright pink or purplish under surface epidermis.

Phase 3 (Weeks 8–16)
Capillary Remodeling

Anti-vascular actives constrict vessel lumen while Centella triterpenes stimulate Type III collagen around vessel walls.
