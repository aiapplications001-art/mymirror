The Molecular Difference: Water-Soluble vs. Oil-Soluble

Both Glycolic Acid and Salicylic Acid are chemical exfoliants, but their chemical structures dictate where and how they operate on your skin:

AHA (Alpha Hydroxy Acid)

Glycolic Acid

The smallest AHA molecule (2 carbons). It is water-soluble, operating on the outer stratum corneum layer to loosen dead skin cells, accelerate cell turnover, and fade dark melanin deposits.

BHA (Beta Hydroxy Acid)

Salicylic Acid

An oil-soluble (lipophilic) acid derived from willow bark. It passes straight through skin lipids and dives deep into pore linings to break down sticky sebum and dead keratin plugs.

💡 The Indian Pigmentation Warning: Indian skin tones (Fitzpatrick IV-VI) produce melanin rapidly in response to micro-inflammation. Over-using strong Glycolic Acid (above 10%) without sun protection or combining it with Salicylic Acid causes chemical irritation, leading to rebound dark spots (PIH).

Which Active Should You Choose?

Match your primary skin symptom to the correct acid:

  • Choose Salicylic Acid if: You have active red pimples, oily T-zone, stubborn blackheads on your nose, or helmet acne mechanica.
  • Choose Glycolic Acid if: Your active breakouts have cleared, but you are left with stubborn brown/black post-acne marks (PIH), dullness, or rough texture.
  • Choose Both (Alternating) if: You have ongoing mild breakouts AND persistent old dark marks—provided your skin barrier is healthy.

The 3-Night Alternating Exfoliation Protocol

To prevent skin barrier damage and chemical burns on Indian skin, follow this alternating schedule:

Night 1: BHA Pore Clear

Salicylic Acid 2% Serum

Apply 2–3 drops of 2% Salicylic Acid to clean, dry skin. Focus on clogged areas (nose, chin, forehead). Seal with a light moisturizer.

Night 2: AHA Brightening

Glycolic Acid 6% Cream / Toner

Apply Glycolic Acid (5–8%) across dark mark zones. Do not use on active open pimples. Follow with a barrier moisturizer.

Night 3: Barrier Rest

Ceramide Recovery Night

Skip all exfoliation. Apply a rich Ceramide & Hyaluronic Acid hydrator to rebuild the intercellular lipid barrier.

Daily Non-Negotiable

SPF 50+ Broad Spectrum

Chemical acids expose fresh epidermal cells. Apply SPF 50 daily to avoid darkening your acne marks under the Indian sun.

Expert FAQ: Glycolic vs. Salicylic Acid
Which acid is better for fading old post-acne dark marks (PIH)? +
Glycolic Acid (AHA) is superior for fading dark marks because its small molecular size penetrates the upper epidermis, dissolving intercellular bonds and accelerating pigment desquamation.
Can I layer Glycolic Acid and Salicylic Acid in the same night routine? +
Dermatologists advise against layering them simultaneously on Indian skin. Combining strong AHA and BHA serums increases transepidermal water loss and skin barrier damage, which triggers post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH).
What is the safest way to use both Glycolic and Salicylic Acid? +
Use the Alternating Night Method: Salicylic Acid 2% on Night 1 for pore clearing, Glycolic Acid 6% on Night 2 for surface brightening, and a Ceramide Hydrating Cream on Night 3 for barrier recovery.
