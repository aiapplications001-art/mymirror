2. Dual Biological Chemistry: Cytokine Suppression & Ceramide Synthesis

Post-acne erythema (PAE) occurs when dilated capillaries and persistent skin inflammation persist after a pimple heals:

Centella Asiatica (Cica) Mechanics
Centella Asiatica contains four active triterpenoid saponins: madecassoside, asiaticoside, madecassic acid, and asiatic acid. Madecassoside down-regulates pro-inflammatory interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) cytokines, instantly constricting micro-vascular flushing and repairing damaged collagen fibers (read our Cica Barrier Guide).

Niacinamide (Vitamin B3) Mechanics
Niacinamide acts as a precursor to NAD(P)H coenzymes, stimulating keratinocytes to synthesize intercellular ceramides, cholesterol, and free fatty acids. Simultaneously, Niacinamide inhibits up to 68% of melanosome transfer from melanocytes into surrounding epidermal cells, preventing red spots from darkening into brown marks (PIH).

3. Functions, Pros & Cons: Cica vs Niacinamide

Compare the primary clinical roles of Centella Asiatica and Niacinamide:

Centella Asiatica (Cica)

Primary Function: Herbal triterpenoid anti-inflammatory that cools acute erythema and speeds up wound healing.

  • ✓ Suppresses IL-6 inflammatory cytokines
  • ✓ Calms burning & stinging from active breakouts
  • ✓ Zero irritation risk across all skin types

✓ Ideal for reactive, red, or compromised skin barriers.

Niacinamide (Vitamin B3)

Primary Function: Ceramide booster & melanosome transfer blocker that strengthens barrier integrity.

  • ✓ Stimulates natural ceramide synthesis
  • ✓ Blocks pigment transfer to prevent dark marks
  • ✓ Regulates sebum production in oily T-zones

❌ Cons: High 10% concentrations can cause temporary flushing in sensitive skin.
