Cetaphil Cleanser for Acne & Oily Skin: Clinical Repair Protocol

In outpatient dermatology practice across North America, differentiating between acute barrier breakdown and primary inflammatory acne represents a critical clinical imperative. Erroneous deployment of high-surfactant soaps over damaged stratum corneum accelerates transepidermal water loss, while substituting physiological barrier repair cleansers for exfoliating actives restores the epidermal acid mantle.

⚠️ Clinical Pharmacotherapy Alert: While acne vulgaris requires regulated follicular desquamation, retinoid-induced xerosis and acid burns stem from stratum corneum lipid depletion. Applying exfoliating acids to a compromised skin barrier provokes contact dermatitis, destroys commensal microflora, and escalates transepidermal water loss across Fitzpatrick phototypes I through VI.

Clinical Phenotypes and Dermoscopic Clues in Acne Vulgaris Patients

Dermoscopic evaluation of active-induced barrier dermatitis versus primary inflammatory acne on sensitive skin presents hurdles, as baseline erythema conceals subtle microcomedones. Whereas superficial pustules in fair phototypes display bright macular redness, irritated patients frequently present with post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation masking deeper follicular inflammation. Under polarized dermoscopy, active bacterial folliculitis demonstrates discrete yellowish pustules with brown-black keratotic plugs, surrounded by peripheral vascular loops. Conversely, irritant barrier dermatitis from unbuffered foaming washes reliably exhibits diffuse epidermal desquamation, fine polygonal scaling, and marked skin-marking accentuation lacking purulent plugs.

Why does benzoyl peroxide provoke severe peeling when combined with topical retinoids? Benzoyl peroxide operates via decomposition into benzoic acid and free oxygen radicals, conferring bactericidal activity against hypoxic Cutibacterium acnes. When paired with retinoids without a physiological ceramide buffer, generated reactive oxygen species oxidize intercellular lipid bilayers, causing acute barrier breakdown and stinging dysesthesia across phototypes I through VI.

Standardized Dermoscopy and Microscopic Follicular Assessment

How do clinicians differentiate active barrier breakdown from persistent bacterial folliculitis? While Cutibacterium acnes resides deep within the anaerobic infundibulum and is evaluated via microbiological culture or PCR, keratolytic active response is assessed instrumentally via open-chamber corneometry and polarized follicular dermoscopy. A basal transepidermal water loss exceeding 25 grams per square meter per hour confirms stratum corneum barrier compromise characteristic of unbuffered acid xerosis. Clinically, barrier breakdown presents with discrete superficial scales encircling follicular ostia and generalized desquamation strictly devoid of purulent exudate.
