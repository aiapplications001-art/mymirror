1. Is It Fungal Acne (Malassezia Folliculitis)?

Fungal acne is not true acne—it is an overgrowth of Malassezia yeast feeding on skin oils (lipids with carbon chain lengths C12-C24). Key signs include:

  • Uniform, monomorphic small bumps concentrated on forehead, hairline, chest, or back.
  • Itchiness aggravated by sweat, humidity, and post-workout dampness.
  • No response to benzoyl peroxide or topical antibiotics.

2. Dermatologist Anti-Fungal Actives

Ketoconazole (2%)

Gold-standard anti-fungal agent that destroys yeast cell membranes. (e.g. Nizral 2% / Sebizole wash).

Zinc Pyrithione (ZPT 1%)

Potent anti-microbial agent that prevents yeast proliferation while calming scalp dandruff and forehead bumps.

4. Short-Contact Therapy Technique

To maximize anti-fungal efficacy without drying out your facial skin:

  1. Lather Ketoconazole 2% wash onto wet forehead and affected areas.
  2. Leave the foam on for 3 to 5 minutes before rinsing off completely with lukewarm water.
  3. Follow immediately with a 100% fungal-safe gel moisturizer (e.g. Neutrogena Hydro Boost / Excela Gel).

5. Frequently Asked Questions

Can oil cleansing worsen fungal acne?

Yes! Natural plant oils (coconut oil, olive oil, argan oil) feed Malassezia yeast. Use Micellar Water or fungal-safe Caprylic Triglyceride cleansers instead.
