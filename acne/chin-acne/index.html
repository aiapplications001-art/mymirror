Across outpatient dermatology consultations in New Delhi, Mumbai, and Bengaluru, adult women frequently present with recurrent nodulocystic lesions distributed across the mandibular jawline and mental area. Cutaneous pilosebaceous units within the lower third of the face possess high densities of androgen receptors. Intracellular 5-alpha reductase enzymes convert circulating testosterone into dihydrotestosterone, driving basal sebocyte lipid synthesis while accelerating infundibular hyperkeratosis. During late premenstrual phases, relative progesterone fluctuations exacerbate follicular wall edema, trapping microbial colonies within deeper dermal planes.

Differentiating hormonal chin acne from perioral dermatitis remains essential during clinical examination. Perioral eruptions present with pin-point inflammatory microvesicles clustered around the oral commissures, characteristically sparing a narrow vermilion margin. Standard application of leave-on keratolytics rapidly exacerbates perioral barrier erythema, whereas true hormonal acne responds to topical dicarboxylic acids and receptor-selective retinoids.

Dermatologists initiate outpatient management combining topical azelaic acid 15% to 20% gel with evening adapalene 0.1%. Azelaic acid competitively inhibits tyrosinase enzymes to halt post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation on Fitzpatrick phototypes IV through VI, while adapalene normalizes follicular desquamation. For refractory deep cystic nodules, systemic anti-androgen therapy with spironolactone or combined oral contraceptives effectively dampens peripheral sebaceous gland stimulation.
