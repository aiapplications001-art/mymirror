Understanding Tyrosinase Inhibition and Silicone Gel Efficacy

Many commercial "scar removal creams" promise to erase physical, pitted acne scars. However, skin science tells a different story. Over-the-counter creams can only target surface issues: they use tyrosinase inhibitors (like Kojic Acid or Vitamin C) to block melanin synthesis and fade dark spots (PIH), or use silicone gel to lock in hydration and flatten raised keloids.

Topical creams cannot rebuild collagen or repair deep dermal damage. Marketing claims promising to cure boxcar, icepick, or rolling scars with simple creams are misleading.

Topical Active Ingredient Capability Scorecard

Different topical active ingredients target different phases of skin healing and pigmentation. While some are excellent for pigmentation, others do not affect structural scars. Read the scorecard below to understand what topicals can and cannot do.

3
Topical Active Ingredient Capability Scorecard

Use this clinical matrix to evaluate active ingredients, their targeting mechanisms, and their true effectiveness for different scar types:

Active Ingredient Target Mechanism Efficacy: Hyperpigmentation (PIH/PIE) Efficacy: Pitted Scars (Boxcar/Icepick) Clinical Assessment & Core Warnings
Retinoids (Tretinoin / Adapalene) Collagen synthesis & cell turnover Excellent Moderate (Superficial Only) Helps improve light, superficial texture over 6+ months. Has no effect on deep, fibrotic scars.
Kojic Acid / Alpha Arbutin Tyrosinase enzyme inhibition Excellent Poor (No Effect) Effective for fading brown PIH marks. Does not affect dermal collagen or structural tissue.
Silicone Gel / Sheets Occlusion & hydration lock Poor Poor (Raised Scars Only) Highly effective for raised hypertrophic or keloid scars. Does not help depressed pitted scars.
Onion Extract / Allium Cepa Anti-inflammatory hydration Poor Poor (No Effect) Common in drugstore creams, but has weak clinical backing. Offers basic skin hydration only.
4
The Real Solution for Pitted Scars

If you have structural pitted scars, save your money on expensive "removal creams." Instead, consult a certified dermatologist to discuss physical resurfacing treatments (such as microneedling, subcision, or laser resurfacing) that physically stimulate and remodel dermal collagen.

💡 Clinical Tip

Don't fall for "scar removal cream" ads. Structural pits require physical remodeling in the dermis. Use topicals to treat discoloration, not physical tissue holes.
