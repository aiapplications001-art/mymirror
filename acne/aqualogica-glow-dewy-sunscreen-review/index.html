💧

Filter Science

The 6 UV Filters: Is It Chemical or Mineral?

There is a widespread misconception online that Aqualogica Glow+ is a mineral sunscreen. It is 100% chemical (organic) with zero Zinc Oxide and zero Titanium Dioxide. Here is how each filter functions:

1. Avobenzone UVA Defender

The global gold-standard absorber for deep UVA rays. It protects skin cells from photo-aging, loss of elasticity, and post-acne dark spots [2].

2. Mexoryl SX UVA1 & Stabilizer

A patented modern filter that shields against long-wave UVA1 rays while preventing Avobenzone from breaking down in direct sunlight.

3. Tinosorb M Broad-Spectrum Hybrid

A cutting-edge organic micro-fine filter that absorbs UV rays and reflects high-energy visible blue light emitted by digital screens.

4. Octinoxate UVB Sunburn Blocker

A classic, potent UVB filter that prevents daytime skin burning, tanning, and immediate sun damage.

5. Ensulizole Water-Soluble Filter

Dissolves directly in water rather than oil, allowing the sunscreen to feel refreshingly weightless on the face rather than sticky or greasy.

6. Octisalate SPF Booster

Works harmoniously with the other five filters to stabilize the sunscreen film across your face and boost the overall SPF rating [4].

Clinical Caution

3 Real-World Limitations You Must Know

While Aqualogica Glow+ feels amazing upon initial application, our clinical audit reveals three key limitations every shopper should consider.

⚠️

1. Not Sweat or Water Resistant

The sunscreen has not undergone water-immersion testing under ISO 16217 protocols [3]. If you step out into humid Indian summers, walk in the rain, or sweat during a commute, the protective barrier washes away quickly. It is strictly an indoor and light-transit sunscreen.

👁️

2. The Sweaty Eye-Stinging Problem

The formula contains volatile solvents (like isododecane) to keep it lightweight. When you sweat, these light solvents carry organic chemical filters into the periorbital area, causing sharp stinging and watery eyes. Avoid applying it directly on your upper eyelids.

⏳

3. The "12-Hour Protection" Myth

Marketing banners frequently mention 12 hours of hydration, which consumers mistake for 12 hours of sun protection. In reality, all chemical sunscreens degrade under natural daylight and facial oils [1]. You still need to reapply every 2 to 3 hours if sitting near sunny windows or walking outside.

Verdict: Highly Recommended (Glow+ Flagship)

Aqualogica Glow+ Dewy Sunscreen is built for your routine. It blocks harmful indoor blue light (via Tinosorb M), hydrates against AC dryness, and delivers a healthy radiant glow with zero white cast.

Verdict: Choose Radiance+ (Watermelon) Instead

While the Glow+ version won't directly clog pores, its glossy finish can make oily skin look greasy. Swap to the Aqualogica Radiance+ Dewy variant — its 2% Niacinamide formula actively regulates oil flow while remaining water-light.

Verdict: Not Recommended (Pick a Water-Resistant Gel)

Because Glow+ Dewy lacks water resistance, perspiration will wash it off within 30 minutes, leaving your skin unprotected and your eyes stinging. Opt for an anhydrous silicone gel sunscreen (like Re'equil Dry Touch or UV Doux).
