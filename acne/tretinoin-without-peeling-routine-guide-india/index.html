The Science of Retinization: Why Peeling & Irritation Occurs

When starting prescription Tretinoin (0.025% or 0.05%), retinoic acid binds to nuclear receptors in epidermal keratinocytes, causing rapid cell turnover. During the first 2 to 6 weeks (the retinization phase), several physiological changes take place:

1. Temporary Ceramide Dip

Tretinoin temporarily downregulates endogenous ceramide synthesis during the first 14 days, causing stratum corneum desquamation and transepidermal water loss (TEWL).

2. Irregular Corneocyte Shedding

As basal cells divide faster, surface corneocytes detach prematurely in visible white flakes—especially around the mouth and nostrils.

Golden Rule for Indian Skin: Never apply Tretinoin to damp or wet skin! Water lowers skin electrical resistance and dramatically spikes trans-epidermal absorption speed, triggering severe burning, redness, and peeling.

Step-by-Step Application Protocol (The Sandwich Method)

The Sandwich Method buffers retinoic acid penetration, slowing down absorption without compromising collagen-building efficacy:

1
15-Minute Dry Rule

Wash face with a gentle cleanser, towel dry gently, and wait 15 minutes to ensure skin is 100% bone-dry before applying any active product.

Bone-Dry Skin
2
First Buffer Layer

Apply a thin layer of ceramide moisturizer across your face. Allow it to absorb for 5 minutes to create a protective hydration barrier.

Buffer Layer
3
Pea-Sized Tretinoin

Squeeze a strict 0.25g pea-sized dot of Tretinoin 0.025% onto your fingertip. Dot onto forehead, cheeks, and chin before spreading evenly.

0.25g Pea Dose
4
Second Top Layer

Follow with a second layer of moisturizer to seal in hydration and cushion retinoid action overnight.

Top Seal Layer
