## 2. Target Persona & 'Average Human' Tone Mandate
- **Reader Persona**: An Indian teenager, student, or working adult who started Tretinoin (Retino-A, Tretiheal, or A-Ret) 3 to 6 weeks ago hoping for glass skin, but is now panicking because their face is peeling, red, and has MORE bumps than before. They are one day away from throwing the tube in the dustbin.
- **Tone Guidelines**:
  - Reassuring, empathetic, clear, and completely jargon-free.
  - Explain complex dermatology through everyday life analogies:
    - **The Deep House Cleaning Metaphor (The Purge)**: Tretinoin does not create new pimples; it acts like a broom sweeping dust from deep under the bed. It pushes micro-clogs that were already hiding under the surface out into the open all at once. If you quit at week 4, you stop during the messiest part of the cleaning!
    - **The Ghee/Oil Clog (Cream vs Gel)**: Traditional Tretinoin Cream contains thick binding ingredients like isopropyl myristate (like putting ghee on an oily face). If someone with oily Indian skin uses the cream version, the medicine helps while the cream base clogs pores! Switching to an aqueous gel or microsphere gel fixes this immediately.
    - **The Sponge Trap (Wet Skin Rule)**: Applying tretinoin on damp skin is like pouring water on a dry sponge—it absorbs the acid in a sudden flash flood, burning the skin barrier. Skin must be 100% dry.
    - **The Sandwich Method**: Moisturizer -> Pea-sized Tretinoin -> Moisturizer. Cushioning the skin so it stays calm and clear.
  - **The 7 Core Reasons Tretinoin 'Fails'**:
    1. Quitting during the 12-week purge (impatience)
    2. Using the Cream version on oily skin (vehicle comedogenicity)
    3. Applying on wet or damp skin (flash absorption burning)
    4. Using too much (the dime vs pea mistake)
    5. Incompatibility with morning actives (mixing with benzoyl peroxide at the same minute)
    6. Damaged skin barrier (peeling mistaken for acne)
    7. Wrong diagnosis (fungal acne or hormonal cysts that need internal treatment)
