# Pre-Writing Strategy: PIE vs. PIH on Brown Skin (Red Marks vs. Dark Spots)

**Topic:** Distinguishing between Post-Inflammatory Erythema (PIE) and Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH) for Indian skin tones (Fitzpatrick Types IV–VI), including clinical definitions, diagnostic hacks, and ingredient playbooks.
**Primary Keyword:** `pie vs pih indian skin`
**Target Audience:** Indian skincare users (ages 18–35) seeking to clear lingering post-acne marks who are confused about why standard fading serums (like Glycolic or Salicylic Acid) are making their red/purple marks worse.

## 1. Editorial Empathy & Voice
- **Voice:** Authoritative clinical advisor, accessible, direct, and reassuring.
- **Hook:** "You finally got your active breakouts under control, but your face is still covered in a map of red, purple, and brown marks. You buy a bottle of high-strength Glycolic Acid or Vitamin C to 'peel' them away, but three weeks in, the marks are darker and your skin is burning. Why? Because you treated Post-Inflammatory Erythema (vascular red marks) as if it were Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (melanin dark spots). On Indian skin, mistreating these marks is the fastest route to permanent discoloration. Let's fix that."
- **Empathy Point:** Address the frustration of having "clear" skin that still looks broken out because of post-inflammatory marks. Explain that skin types IV–VI react uniquely to inflammation, making accurate diagnosis critical.

## 2. Key Pillars & Content Points
- **Post-Inflammatory Erythema (PIE) Explained:**
    - **What it is:** Vascular damage. Microcapillaries (tiny blood vessels) under the skin dilate or rupture during acne inflammation, trapping red blood cells near the surface.
    - **Color on Indian Skin:** Red, pink, or deep purple.
    - **The Blanch Test Hack:** Press a clear glass slide or clean finger against the mark. If it temporarily fades/turns white (blanches), it is PIE (vascular).
    - **Treatment Rule:** Absolutely NO harsh exfoliating acids. Treat it with soothing, vascular-constricting, and barrier-repairing ingredients: **Centella Asiatica (Cica)**, **Niacinamide (2–5%)**, **Panthenol (Pro-Vitamin B5)**, and **Allantoin**.
- **Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH) Explained:**
    - **What it is:** Melanin overproduction. Melanocytes (pigment cells) are triggered by inflammation to produce excess melanin, which drops into the deeper layers of the epidermis.
    - **Color on Indian Skin:** Brown, dark brown, or black.
    - **The Blanch Test Hack:** Press against the mark. If it stays exactly the same color (does not blanch), it is PIH (pigment).
    - **Treatment Rule:** Use tyrosinase-inhibitors and cell-turnover enhancers to break down the pigment: **Tranexamic Acid**, **Alpha Arbutin**, **Kojic Acid**, **Vitamin C**, and **Retinoids (Adapalene)**.
- **The "Indian Climate" Influence:** Strong UV levels in India aggressively darken both PIE and PIH. Unprotected sun exposure will convert temporary vascular PIE into deep, stubborn PIH. Sunscreen is non-negotiable.
