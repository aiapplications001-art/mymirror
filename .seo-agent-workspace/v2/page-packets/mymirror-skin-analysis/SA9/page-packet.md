### S1_hero: Milia on face: tiny white bumps or something else?

Milia-like bumps are easy to confuse with whiteheads, clogged pores, or flat white spots. Start with whether the mark is raised, firm, and not inflamed.

### S2_visible_clues: Do not squeeze first

Milia are commonly described as small white cyst-like bumps under the skin, but not every small white bump is milia. A photo can help you compare location and texture; it cannot confirm what the bump is.

### S3_scan_limits: What the scan can and cannot do

What MyMirror can help organize: Use the scan to separate raised white bumps from flat pale patches, redness, acne clusters, and visible pores.

What it cannot decide: A scan cannot confirm milia, diagnose cysts, or decide whether extraction is appropriate.

Safer next step: Avoid piercing or squeezing at home, especially near the eye. Keep products simple and ask a professional if bumps persist or bother you.

### S4_escalation: When to stop guessing

Get medical advice for bumps near the eye, painful or changing lesions, or anything you are unsure about before attempting removal.

### S5_faq: Questions people ask

Q: Are milia the same as whiteheads?
A: No. They can look similar, but milia are not simply pimples waiting to pop.

Q: Will milia go away by themselves?
A: Some can fade over time, but persistent adult milia may need professional advice.
