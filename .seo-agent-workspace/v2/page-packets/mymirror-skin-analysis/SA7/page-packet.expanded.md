## Page Sections

### S1_hero: White spots on face: what should you observe first?

White spots can mean different things depending on whether they are flat, raised, scaly, spreading, or linked to dryness. Start by describing what is visible instead of naming a condition too soon.

### S2_visible_clues: Notice whether the spot is flat or raised

A flat pale patch, a tiny raised bump, and a flaky lighter area are different visual patterns. The goal is not to self-diagnose. It is to gather a clearer description so your next step is more sensible.

### S3_scan_limits: What the scan can and cannot do

What MyMirror can help organize: Use a scan to map where the lighter areas appear and whether they look flat, raised, or texture-led.

What it cannot decide: A scan cannot distinguish vitiligo, fungal changes, pityriasis alba, post-inflammatory color loss, or other conditions with certainty.

Safer next step: Avoid squeezing, scraping, or bleaching the area. Use gentle care, daily sun protection, and get medical advice for spreading or symptomatic patches.

### S4_escalation: When to stop guessing

Seek professional care if white patches spread, itch, hurt, scale heavily, appear after a new medicine, or affect confidence enough that you need a clear diagnosis.

### S5_faq: Questions people ask

Q: Are all white spots on the face milia?
A: No. Milia are raised tiny cyst-like bumps. Flat pale patches have a different set of possibilities.

Q: Can a scan tell me if it is vitiligo?
A: No. MyMirror can organize visible patterns, but diagnosis belongs with a dermatologist.

Q: Should I exfoliate white spots?
A: Do not treat first and identify later. If the area is irritated, exfoliation can make it worse.

### S6_final_cta: Use the scan to describe, not diagnose

Start with what is visible today, choose one sensible next step, and get professional care when needed.
