# Pre-Writing Strategy: Can You Analyse My Face?

## Page decision

- Page ID: FA1
- Slug: `can-you-analyse-my-face`
- Page type: informational decision guide with a direct scan action
- Primary query: “can you analyse my face and tell me what problems I have?”
- Audience: people who can see more than one facial-skin concern and want a calm first step before changing their routine.
- Tone: calm, transparent, practical. The user approved a scan-first CTA and the exact hero proof line.
- Depth: 1,500–1,900 visible-body words.

## Search and evidence basis

Current search results show that people expect an instant read of visible concerns, but trustworthy tools distinguish cosmetic observation from medical diagnosis. MyMirror’s published homepage positions the scan as a visible-signal map, not a beauty score or diagnosis. The American Academy of Dermatology’s photo guidance supports clear, well-lit photos as useful documentation, while its acne guidance illustrates that similar-looking concerns may need clinical assessment. NHS guidance supports escalating a new or changing mole or other worrying change rather than relying on a photo tool.

Sources reviewed:

- MyMirror home page — product boundary, no-account and 60-second positioning.
- American Academy of Dermatology: [how to take pictures of your skin](https://www.aad.org/public/fad/digital-health/taking-pictures-skin) — clear, consistent images help show colour and texture.
- American Academy of Dermatology: [is that stubborn acne really acne?](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/acne/really-acne/stubborn-acne) — acne-like concerns can have other explanations.
- NHS: [moles](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/moles/) — new or changing moles warrant clinical review.

## Unique reader value

Most face-scan pages jump from selfie to an overconfident list of “problems.” This page makes the useful distinction: a photo can organize visible patterns by facial area, but cannot name their cause. Its standout device is a five-area face map that translates “what I see” into a sensible next step while keeping escalation guidance visible.

## Page structure and evidence duties

1. Hero — answer the query directly, set visible-not-diagnostic scope, and show the user-approved proof line.
2. Quick answer — distinguish visible patterns from medical diagnosis.
3. Five-area face map — foreground, nose/T-zone, cheeks, jaw/chin, eye area; use observation language only.
4. What makes a selfie useful — even light, neutral expression, no beauty filters; cite AAD photo context.
5. Signal-to-next-step matrix — visible cue, calm first action, boundary that requires professional input.
6. Why the result is not a beauty score — reduce shame/product pressure and clarify the scan’s decision role.
7. When a scan is not the right next step — urgent and clinically sensitive examples; cite AAD/NHS.
8. Photo preparation — practical checklist.
9. FAQ — privacy, scan limits, accuracy boundary, results, makeup, and dermatology handoff.
10. Final CTA — send to `/scan`, not to a product list.

## Guardrails

- Do not claim that MyMirror diagnoses, detects, grades severity, or treats a condition.
- Do not mention competitor brands, use third-party product imagery, or use externally branded visuals.
- Avoid product recommendations and medical claims that would require product-specific evidence.
- Keep the hero proof line exactly: “Free to start · About 60 seconds · No account needed · Trusted by 50,000+ users”.
- Use the existing MyMirror hero artwork and orange editorial system.
