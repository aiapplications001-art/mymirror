# SC1 — What Is Wrong With My Skin?

## Search intent and page job

**Primary query:** “what is wrong with my skin?”
**Intent:** anxious, broad informational self-triage. Visitors want a useful way to describe what changed before deciding whether to simplify a routine, use MyMirror’s visible-pattern scan, or seek clinical care. The page must not diagnose.

## Audience and promise

People who have noticed a new, persistent, or confusing change on facial skin—breakouts, dark marks, oiliness, texture, flaking, redness or sensitivity—often after a product, weather, routine, shaving, hair-product, sleep, stress or cycle change. The page offers a calm framework: appearance, sensation, timing and location.

## Differentiation

This is not a reworded face-analysis landing page. The existing face page explains what a scan can observe by face area. This page is a broader *concern-clarity* guide organised around a four-clue triage, a change timeline, a product-routine audit, practical skin-tone context, and a decision path.

## Required experience

## Evidence ledger

1. American Academy of Dermatology (AAD), [taking pictures of your skin](https://www.aad.org/public/fad/digital-health/taking-pictures-skin): clear, evenly lit photos are more useful in teledermatology contexts. Use for photo guidance.
2. AAD, [maximizing results from skin-care products](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/anti-aging/maximize-anti-aging-products): introduce products thoughtfully; stop products that burn, sting or tingle. Use for routine-audit safety.
3. AAD, [acne in darker skin tones](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/acne/DIY/skin-color): acne can be accompanied by lingering hyperpigmentation in skin of color. Use for tone-aware context.
4. AAD, [rosacea overview](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/rosacea/what-is/overview): redness can appear brown or violet in darker skin; burning or stinging can matter. Use only to explain why a broad visual category is not a diagnosis.
5. NHS, [moles](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/moles/): changing, itchy, bleeding, crusty or otherwise unusual moles warrant clinical advice. Use in escalation section.
