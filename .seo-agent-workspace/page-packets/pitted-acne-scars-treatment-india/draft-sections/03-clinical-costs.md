## Clinical Gold Standards for Indian Skin

Treating scars on Indian skin (Fitzpatrick IV-VI) requires a delicate balance. High-energy lasers that work on Caucasian skin can cause **Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH)** in darker tones. 2026 clinical standards favor treatments that bypass the surface of the skin to target the deeper dermis.

### 1. MNRF (Microneedling Radiofrequency)
**The Safety Hero.** MNRF uses insulated needles to deliver radiofrequency heat directly into the dermis. Because the surface layer (epidermis) is spared from the heat, it is exceptionally safe for brown skin.
- **Best for:** Boxcar and Rolling scars.
- **Downtime:** 24-48 hours of redness.

### 2. Fractional CO2 Laser
**The Resurfacer.** This laser creates thousands of microscopic "holes" in the skin to force it to rebuild. While powerful, it requires a "lower fluence" setting for Indian skin to avoid pigment damage.
- **Best for:** Overall texture smoothing and Boxcar scars.
- **Downtime:** 5-7 days of "grid-like" scabbing.

### 3. Subcision
**The Liberator.** Rolling scars are often "tethered" to the underlying muscle by thick bands of fibrous tissue. Subcision involves using a specialized needle to "cut" these bands, allowing the skin to lift and smooth out.
- **Best for:** Rolling scars.
- **Downtime:** 3-5 days of bruising.

### 4. TCA CROSS
**The Closer.** Trichloroacetic Acid (TCA) Chemical Reconstruction of Skin Scars (CROSS) involves placing high-concentration acid directly inside deep pits to trigger localized collagen growth.
- **Best for:** Ice-pick and narrow Boxcar scars.
- **Downtime:** Scabs in the pits for 7-10 days.
