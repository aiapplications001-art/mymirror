## Can You Treat Pitted Scars at Home? (The Truth)

If you've spent thousands on "scar removal creams" only to see no change in your pits, you aren't alone. **Topical creams cannot replace lost tissue.** However, they play a vital "support" role in scar revision.

### What At-Home Care CAN Do:
- **Smooth Surface Texture:** Ingredients like **Tretinoin** or **Adapalene** increase cell turnover, making rolling scars look slightly softer.
- **Pre-Condition the Skin:** Most Indian dermatologists will put you on a "Tyrosinase Inhibitor" (Kojic Acid, Tranexamic Acid) for 4 weeks before a laser session to prevent PIH.
- **Maintain Results:** Using **Copper Peptides** or **Matrixyl 3000** at home can support the collagen-building process initiated in the clinic.

### What At-Home Care CANNOT Do:
- **Close Ice-Pick Scars:** These are too narrow and deep for any cream to penetrate effectively.
- **Lift Tethered Scars:** No topical can cut the internal fibrous bands that hold rolling scars down.
- **Provide Instant Fill:** If a product claims to "fill craters" overnight, it is likely using silicone to temporarily smooth the surface, not rebuilding skin.

## Clinical FAQ: Expert Answers for Indian Skin

### 1. Is it too late to treat my 10-year-old acne scars?
No. While younger scars (less than 1-2 years old) have a slightly better "healing capacity," mature scars can still be significantly improved using combination therapy like Subcision and MNRF.

### 2. Will lasers burn or darken my brown skin?
In the hands of a qualified dermatologist using the right settings, the risk is minimal. However, Indian skin *must* be treated with caution. This is why MNRF is often preferred over CO2 lasers for Fitzpatrick IV-VI skin types.

### 3. How much improvement should I realistically expect?
Total "erasure" of scars is rare. A realistic goal is **50% to 80% improvement**. At this level, scars are no longer visible in most lighting conditions and can be easily covered with light makeup.

### 4. Can I use a dermaroller at home?
We strongly advise against it for deep pits. At-home dermarollers often have poor-quality needles that can cause "micro-tearing" and more scarring. Clinical-grade dermapens or MNRF are far safer and more effective.
