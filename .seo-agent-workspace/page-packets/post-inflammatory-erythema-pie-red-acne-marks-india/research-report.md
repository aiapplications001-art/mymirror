# Clinical Research Report: Post-Inflammatory Erythema (PIE) in Indian Dermatology

## 1. Pathophysiology: Vascular Erythema vs Melanin Hyperpigmentation
- **Post-Inflammatory Erythema (PIE)** represents discrete, erythematous (pink to deep red/violaceous) macules caused by microvascular dilation, damage, and pooling of oxygenated hemoglobin within the papillary and superficial reticular dermis following an inflammatory acne lesion.
- **Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH)**, in contrast, results from melanocyte hyperactivity stimulated by inflammatory cytokines (prostaglandins, leukotrienes), leading to excessive melanin deposition in the basal layer of the epidermis or dermal melanophages.
- While PIH is widely recognized on Fitzpatrick skin phototypes III–VI, PIE is increasingly documented in Indian patients, particularly on the cheeks and jawline following cystic eruptions or mechanical manipulation (picking/squeezing).

## 2. The Diascopy Test (Glass Test) Diagnostic Mechanism
- Diascopy relies on applying direct perpendicular pressure with a transparent glass slide or clean tumbler against the erythematous lesion.
- External pressure overcomes the hydrostatic pressure of the dilated capillary lumen, blanching the lesion completely (positive diascopy = PIE).
- Melanin pigment is non-vascular; therefore, a pure PIH macule fails to blanch under diascopy. Mixed lesions (PIE + PIH) demonstrate partial blanching with residual tan/brown discoloration.

## 3. Topical Pharmacology & Vascular Endothelial Repair
- **Centella Asiatica (Madecassoside, Asiaticoside)**: Promotes microvascular integrity by upregulating collagen type I synthesis and inhibiting excessive vascular permeability.
- **Tranexamic Acid (TXA)**: A synthetic lysine analog that competitively inhibits plasminogen activation. Plasmin stimulates vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and endothelin-1; blocking this cascade decreases microvascular dilation and suppresses secondary melanocyte activation.
- **Azelaic Acid (10%–15%)**: A dicarboxylic acid that inhibits neutrophil-derived reactive oxygen species, suppresses microvascular swelling, and acts as a competitive inhibitor of tyrosinase.
- **Mineral Zinc Oxide**: Provides broad-spectrum physical reflection of UVA and visible light without generating the exothermic heat associated with chemical organic UV filters that exacerbates capillary erythema.
