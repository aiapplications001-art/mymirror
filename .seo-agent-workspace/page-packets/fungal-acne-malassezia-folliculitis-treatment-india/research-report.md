# Research Report: Malassezia Folliculitis (Fungal Acne) on Indian Skin

## 1. Clinical Microbiology & Pathophysiology
- **Organism**: *Malassezia globosa*, *Malassezia restricta*, and *Malassezia sympodialis*—dimorphic, lipid-dependent basidiomycetous yeasts naturally present on the human stratum corneum.
- **Follicular Dysbiosis**: In healthy skin, Malassezia coexists harmoniously with *Cutibacterium acnes* and coagulase-negative staphylococci. When microenvironmental conditions shift—elevated temperature, high ambient relative humidity (>75%), excessive sebum excretion, or antibiotic suppression of bacterial flora—Malassezia shifts from harmless commensal yeast to hyphal/pathogenic forms, triggering perifollicular inflammation, microabscesses, and itchy papulopustules.
- **Lipid Dependency (C11–C24)**: Malassezia organisms lack fatty acid synthase. They require exogenous medium- and long-chain fatty acids (chain lengths C11 through C24) to construct cell membranes. Topical exposure to vegetable oils (coconut oil, olive oil, almond oil), esters (isopropyl myristate, isopropyl palmitate), polysorbates, and fatty acids directly accelerates yeast colony growth.

## 2. Indian Climate, Cultural Triggers & Misdiagnosis
- **Tropical Monsoons & Coastal Humidity**: Cities like Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, and Kochi experience prolonged periods of extreme humidity and warmth, precipitating extensive follicular occlusion.
- **Traditional Scalp Oiling (*Champi*)**: Application of coconut, mustard, or sesame oil to the scalp frequently migrates to the forehead and temples, providing direct nourishment to Malassezia colonies.
- **Gym Culture & Synthetic Fabrics**: Occlusive polyester sportswear traps sweat and sebum against the chest and upper back, creating an ideal incubation microclimate.
- **Antibiotic Paradox**: Patients prescribed oral cyclines (doxycycline, minocycline) or topical clindamycin for presumed inflammatory acne often experience acute worsening with intense itching as competitive bacterial pressure is removed.

## 3. Evidence-Based Topical & Systemic Therapeutics
- **Ketoconazole (2%)**: Imidazole antifungal that blocks fungal ergosterol synthesis via inhibition of cytochrome P450 14-alpha-demethylase. Short-contact application (3 to 5 minutes before rinsing) provides potent fungicidal activity without barrier disruption.
- **Zinc Pyrithione (1%–2%)**: Disables yeast membrane transport and energy metabolism.
- **Selenium Sulfide (2.5%)**: Reduces corneocyte turnover and displays antifungal efficacy against Pityrosporum species.
- **Squalane (Pure Plant-Derived)**: Hydrocarbon oil that does NOT contain the ester linkages or carbon chain configurations utilized by Malassezia, making it completely yeast-safe.
- **Oral Antifungals (Fluconazole, Itraconazole)**: Reserved for severe, recalcitrant, or widespread chest/back folliculitis under strict board-certified dermatological supervision.
