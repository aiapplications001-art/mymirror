# Clinical Research Report: Oral Doxycycline in Indian Acne Practice

## 1. Mechanism of Action
- Broad-spectrum bacteriostatic tetracycline antibiotic.
- Inhibits protein synthesis by binding reversibly to the 30S ribosomal subunit of *Cutibacterium acnes*.
- Independent anti-inflammatory mechanism: inhibits matrix metalloproteinases (MMP-8, MMP-13), suppresses interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β), tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), and reactive oxygen species generation.
- Effective at sub-antimicrobial doses (40mg modified release) and conventional doses (50mg–100mg once or twice daily).

## 2. Gut-Skin Axis & Probiotic Synergy
- Oral antibiotics cause broad depletion of intestinal microbiota, notably *Bifidobacterium* and *Lactobacillus* species.
- Antibiotic-associated dysbiosis triggers gut barrier permeability, intestinal cramping, and diarrhea in up to 30% of patients, leading to premature discontinuation.
- Fixed-dose combination with *Lactic Acid Bacillus* (Doxt-SL) delivers lactic-acid-producing spores that withstand gastric acidity and replenish beneficial gut flora, ensuring high patient compliance.

## 3. Phototoxicity & Ethnic Skin Considerations
- Doxycycline is a dose-dependent phototoxic agent that accumulates in epidermal and dermal cells.
- Absorption spectrum corresponds to UVA radiation (320–400 nm).
- On Indian skin phototypes (Fitzpatrick III–VI), phototoxic injury manifests as intense burning, stinging, and persistent post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) rather than bright erythema.
- Chemical sunscreens can generate thermal byproduct that aggravates sensitized skin; mineral Zinc Oxide physical blockers are clinically mandated.
