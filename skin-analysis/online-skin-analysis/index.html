What an online skin analysis is actually for

A face scan is useful when you can see a few things at once but do not know which deserves attention first. It turns visible patterns into a more structured starting point. It does not tell you what condition you have, replace a dermatologist, or promise a perfect result.

See it by region

Separate what looks most noticeable on your forehead, cheeks, nose, chin and other facial areas rather than treating your whole face as one problem.

Know what to focus on first

Use the result to decide whether visible breakouts, oil balance, marks, texture or sensitivity is the more useful first conversation with your routine.

Choose one next step

A useful scan should reduce product guessing. It can help you start simpler, read a relevant guide, or recognise when professional advice is the better next move.

From your face to a more useful plan

The process is deliberately short. You are not asked to become your own dermatologist—you are simply given a clearer view of visible signals.

Two moments when a scan can be useful

“My forehead is shiny, but my cheeks do not feel the same.”

Instead of buying a stronger cleanser because one area looks oily, start by seeing the pattern by region. The practical decision may be to simplify first, not add three new products.

“The breakouts have settled, but I still see marks and uneven texture.”

A visible map can help you describe what you are seeing and choose a relevant guide. If changes are painful, sudden, persistent or worrying, skip the self-analysis loop and seek professional advice.

“I have read too many conflicting routines.”

A scan is not a verdict. It is a way to stop treating every concern at once and make one decision that fits what you can see today.

When to scan—and when not to

The most responsible skin tool tells you its boundary. A scan can organize visible signals; it cannot diagnose a skin condition or replace a clinical examination.

Start a scan if you want to:
  • understand visible patterns before changing a routine
  • prioritize one concern instead of chasing every trend
  • get a clearer starting point for a skincare guide
Speak to a dermatologist if you have:
  • painful, rapidly changing, bleeding or persistent concerns
  • a question about diagnosis, prescription treatment or a procedure

Questions people ask before a face scan

Does MyMirror diagnose skin conditions?

No. MyMirror helps you understand visible skin patterns and routine priorities. It does not diagnose conditions or replace a qualified dermatologist.

Do I need an account?

No. You can begin the scan without creating an account.

What happens to my scan data?

MyMirror states that scan data is deleted after analysis. Read the current privacy information before using any online service if you have questions about data handling.

What should I do with my result?

Use it to choose one useful next step. That may mean simplifying your routine, reading a relevant MyMirror guide, or sharing the concern with a dermatologist.
